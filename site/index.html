Controlling Untrusted AI:
A Survey

Deployed AI agents must meet their operator's requirements: be correct, compliant, in scope, within budget, and safe. They cannot be trusted to meet them on their own: they make mistakes, they can be redirected by what they read, and they may pursue objectives of their own.

      The Mechanism Map

      Two levers act on an untrusted agent. The first changes the deployment around it, the second changes the model. Verify tests either lever, and deployment and assurance compose the mechanisms into a stack.

      Two-level test. L1, does it still work if the agent is indifferent to the requirement. L2, does it still work if the agent tries to defeat it and knows how it works.

      Who This Is For

      Researchers

      Researchers inside one mechanism family (agent security: injection defences, isolation, information-flow control; guardrails; monitoring; control evaluation; formal enforcement; post-training; steering) who want the other families on one map with the same five questions answered.

      Deploying Teams

      Teams deploying agents with real credentials who must choose and combine mechanisms for a requirement and a threat model.

      Evaluators and Policy Staff

      Evaluators, auditors, and policy staff who need to know what evidence a control claim rests on and which mechanisms satisfy which governance requirements (human oversight, logging, risk management, robustness).
