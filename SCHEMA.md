# Data contracts for `site/data/`

All files are UTF-8 JSON, generated by `pipeline/build_data.py` (taxonomy, papers, grey, stats,
stream_recent) or by `pipeline/collect_news.py` / the seed sweep (stream,
stream_recent, the `stream_*` fields of stats). The front-end reads only these files. Field names
below are final; add fields only by extending this document.

Extended 2026-10-17 (review fixes): `grey.json` `also`, `confidence` closed set, `url` fallbacks;
`stats.json` `source_generated` and the `stream_*` counts; `stream.json` `taxonomy_generated`; the new
`stream_recent.json`.

Changed 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-21 (paper restructured; sources moved to the paper's tooling
repository): 11 sections in four `parts`, 35 subsections; the failure sources are the three failure
tiers `benign | external | insider` (field name unchanged); the operations are the section titles
`constrain | observe | intervene | steer | train | verify | deploy`, one per section (`deploy` is
Section 10, Deployment and Assurance); `lever` is the part title; `sections[].operation` became the
list `operations`; subsection `bullets` and section `purpose` dropped (writer notes);
`taxonomy.structure` and `stream.taxonomy_structure` fingerprint the subsection list. The stream was
re-filed from the 17 October seed classification by `pipeline/remap_stream.py`.

## `taxonomy.json`

```json
{
  "title": "Controlling Untrusted AI: A Survey",
  "thesis": "<one paragraph from outline.json>",
  "readers": ["...", "...", "..."],
  "generated": "2026-10-21",
  "structure": "1ef18ee6f954",
  "requirements": [
    {"id": "correct", "label": "Correct"}, {"id": "compliant", "label": "Compliant"},
    {"id": "in-scope", "label": "In scope"}, {"id": "within-budget", "label": "Within budget"},
    {"id": "safe", "label": "Safe"}
  ],
  "failure_sources": [
    {"id": "benign", "label": "Benign", "color": "#D95358", "blurb": "..."},
    {"id": "external", "label": "External", "color": "#E09A3B", "blurb": "..."},
    {"id": "insider", "label": "Insider", "color": "#A37CAD", "blurb": "..."}
  ],
  "operations": [
    {"id": "failures", "label": "How Agents Fail", "section": "s2", "color": "#D95358", "lever": null},
    {"id": "constrain", "label": "Constrain", "section": "s4", "color": "#E27553", "lever": "Control Outside the Model"},
    {"id": "observe", "label": "Observe", "section": "s5", "color": "#598DD2", "lever": "Control Outside the Model"},
    {"id": "intervene", "label": "Intervene", "section": "s6", "color": "#E09A3B", "lever": "Control Outside the Model"},
    {"id": "steer", "label": "Steer", "section": "s7", "color": "#65C2B9", "lever": "Control Inside the Model"},
    {"id": "train", "label": "Train", "section": "s8", "color": "#8D86E8", "lever": "Control Inside the Model"},
    {"id": "verify", "label": "Verify", "section": "s9", "color": "#62A16E", "lever": "Verification, Deployment, and Outlook"},
    {"id": "deploy", "label": "Deploy", "section": "s10", "color": "#A37CAD", "lever": "Verification, Deployment, and Outlook"}
  ],
  "parts": [
    {"title": "The Problem", "sections": ["s1", "s2", "s3"]},
    {"title": "Control Outside the Model", "sections": ["s4", "s5", "s6"]},
    {"title": "Control Inside the Model", "sections": ["s7", "s8"]},
    {"title": "Verification, Deployment, and Outlook", "sections": ["s9", "s10", "s11"]}
  ],
  "sections": [
    {
      "id": "s4", "number": 4, "title": "Constrain", "slug": "constrain",
      "operations": ["constrain"], "color": "#E27553", "part": 1,
      "subsections": [
        {
          "id": "s4_1", "number": "4.1", "title": "Confinement", "slug": "confinement",
          "counts": {"primary": 56, "selected": 6, "cross": 21, "grey": 1}
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

`structure` is twelve hex digits of SHA-1 over the ordered subsection ids and titles
(`build_data.structure_fingerprint`): two outlines of one day get different fingerprints, and the
collector and the tests compare it with `stream.taxonomy_structure`. `failure_sources` are the three
failure tiers of the paper (Section 2); the blurbs are the paper's one-sentence definitions.
`operations` has one entry per operation in section order, one operation per section; the ids are the
section titles (`deploy` for Deployment and Assurance). `label` is the chip label (`failures` takes
its section title), `color` is the section's colour and `lever` is the title of the section's part,
`null` for `failures`, which is not a control operation. `parts` lists the paper's parts in order
with their section ids; `sections[].part` is the index into `parts`. `sections[].operations` lists
the section's operation ids (`[]` for Sections 1, 3 and 11, which have `color: "#7D7D7D"`). Section titles select operation, slug and colour (`SECTION_META` in
`build_data.py`), so a renumbered outline cannot mislabel a section. `counts.primary` = papers whose
`primary` is this subsection; `selected` = of those, cited in the PDF; `cross` = papers and reports
that list this subsection in `also`; `grey` = grey items whose `primary` is this subsection. A
subsection may have zero primary papers (1.3, 1.4 and 3.3 in the 21 October outline); the front-end
renders the head with an empty state.

## `papers.json`  (array, one object per arXiv paper; 1,788 items)

```json
{
  "key": "cao2026beyond",
  "arxiv_id": "2603.03116",
  "url": "https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03116",
  "title": "Beyond Task Completion: ...",
  "authors": ["Hongliu Cao", "Ilias Driouich", "Eoin Thomas"],
  "year": 2026,
  "primary": "s1_1",
  "section": "s1",
  "score": 3,
  "selected": false,
  "label": "Procedure-Aware Evaluation",
  "one_line": "Procedure-aware evaluation on tau-bench shows ...",
  "reason": "27-78% of reported successes conceal policy violations",
  "also": [{"sid": "s2_1", "score": 3}],
  "reading_rank": 5
}
```

`label` = `labels_method_style.json[key]` else `short_label` else text before the first colon of the
title. `one_line` may be empty. `authors` is the full list (the UI truncates). `year` is an integer.
Sort order in the file: by `primary` (section order), then `selected` desc, `score` desc,
`reading_rank` asc (nulls last), `year` desc, `title`.

## `grey.json`  (array; union of `corpus_full.grey` and `grey_v2.entries`, deduplicated by URL/title)

```json
{
  "key": "greenblatt2025controlmeasures",
  "title": "An overview of control measures",
  "authors": ["Ryan Greenblatt"],
  "year": 2025,
  "venue": "AI Alignment Forum / LessWrong (Redwood Research)",
  "url": "https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/...",
  "primary": "s7_2",
  "section": "s7",
  "label": "Redwood: Control Measures",
  "one_line": "Catalogue of core and long-tail control measures ...",
  "confidence": "verified",
  "selected": true,
  "also": [{"sid": "s10_3", "score": null}]
}
```

`selected` = true when the item is in `corpus_full.grey` (cited in the PDF). `confidence` is one of
`verified | likely | unverified` (the source note "existing bib entry" maps to `verified`; the front-end
badges only `likely` and `unverified`). `url` is never empty: bib entries without a URL take the DOI or
publisher page from `GREY_URL_FALLBACK` in `build_data.py`. One record per document: when two bib keys
share a URL the first key is kept and the second key's subsection becomes an `also` entry with
`score: null`; `also` is otherwise `[]`. A grey item that is the same work as an arXiv paper (same bib
key, or its `arxiv_id` is in `papers.json`) is not emitted: the paper is marked `selected` and the
grey subsection is added to the paper's `also`.

## `stats.json`

```json
{
  "generated": "2026-10-21",
  "source_generated": "2026-10-20",
  "papers": 1788, "grey": 164, "cited": 239, "subsections": 35, "sections": 11,
  "years": {"2026": 908, "2025": 417, "...": 0},
  "per_section": {"s2": 190, "...": 0},
  "stream_items": 231, "stream_updated": "2026-10-17T00:00:00Z",
  "stream_polarity": {"good": 93, "bad": 97, "mixed": 41},
  "stream_per_source": {"benign": 40, "external": 71, "insider": 47},
  "stream_per_op": {"constrain": 149, "deploy": 85, "...": 0},
  "stream_per_sub": {"s1_1": 2, "...": 0}
}
```

`source_generated` is `corpus_full.json.generated`, or the file's modification date when that is later
(the corpus can be re-filed without a new stamp).
`cited` counts distinct cited documents (selected papers plus selected grey records after the merges
above). The `stream_*` counts are derived from `stream.json` by
`build_data.stream_derivatives()` and rewritten by the collector on every run; the landing page reads
them instead of `stream.json`.

## `stream_recent.json`

`{"generated": "<stream.json generated>", "items": [...]}`: the twelve newest items of `stream.json`,
same item schema, in the file's order (date desc, then id desc). Written by `build_data.py`, the
collector and `remap_stream.py`. The landing page
reads this file and falls back to `stream.json` when it is absent.

## `stream.json`  (the live stream)

```json
{
  "generated": "2026-10-17T12:00:00Z",
  "taxonomy_generated": "2026-10-21",
  "taxonomy_structure": "1ef18ee6f954",
  "sources": ["seed sweep 2026-10-17", "collector"],
  "items": [
    {
      "id": "2025-07-18-replit-agent-deleted-production-database",
      "date": "2025-07-18",
      "date_precision": "day",
      "title": "Replit's coding agent deleted a production database during a code freeze",
      "url": "https://...",
      "also_urls": ["https://..."],
      "publisher": "Fortune",
      "kind": "incident",
      "polarity": "bad",
      "summary": "Two factual sentences taken from the source, no speculation.",
      "failure_sources": ["benign"],
      "requirements": ["in-scope", "correct"],
      "operations": ["constrain", "intervene"],
      "subsections": ["s2_1", "s4_1", "s6_3"],
      "domain": "coding",
      "actors": {"vendor": "Replit", "deployer": "SaaStr", "model": null},
      "evidence": "first-party",
      "related_papers": ["khan2026token", "rahman2026locating"],
      "tags": ["destructive action", "rollback"],
      "verified": true,
      "verified_on": "2026-10-17",
      "verification_note": "URL fetched; date and claims confirmed from the article body.",
      "added_by": "seed-sweep",
      "lens": "incidents-coding"
    }
  ]
}
```

Enumerations:

- `kind`: `incident` (harm occurred), `near-miss` (caught before harm), `deployment` (an agent put
  into real use, with or without reported controls), `safety-feature` (a vendor or deployer shipped a
  control mechanism), `evaluation` (a published test of deployed or deployable agents, including system
  cards and red-team reports), `research` (a paper or report with direct deployment relevance),
  `policy` (law, regulation, standard, enforcement, lawsuit), `disclosure` (vendor postmortem or security
  advisory).
- `polarity`: `bad` (a failure or harm), `good` (a success or a control that worked or shipped), `mixed`.
- `failure_sources`: subset of `benign | external | insider` (the attack tier: `benign` = the agent
  itself failed, no attacker; `external` = an outsider redirected it through what it read, called, or
  remembered; `insider` = a person with legitimate partial access, or the model itself pursuing its own
  objective); empty for items that are not about a failure.
- `requirements`: subset of `correct | compliant | in-scope | within-budget | safe`.
- `operations`: subset of `constrain | observe | intervene | steer | train | verify | deploy`; the
  operation that failed, was missing, or worked.
- `subsections`: 1 to 4 subsection ids from `taxonomy.json`, most specific first.
- `domain`: free text from a small controlled list: `coding`, `browsing`, `customer-service`,
  `finance`, `email-and-office`, `healthcare`, `legal`, `cyber`, `research`, `infrastructure`,
  `consumer`, `government`, `multi-agent`, `other`.
- `evidence`: `first-party` (the vendor or deployer said so), `press`, `researcher`, `regulator`,
  `court`, `incident-database`.
- `date_precision`: `day | month | year`.
- `also_urls` (optional): further URLs that report the same event. One item per event: the most
  primary source is kept as `url` (vendor disclosure or official report > researcher > incident
  database > press), the others go here, and `related_papers` and `tags` are the union across the
  reports. Absent when the event has a single source; never empty. Dedupe must consider these URLs too.

`taxonomy_generated` is the `generated` date and `taxonomy_structure` the `structure` fingerprint of
the `taxonomy.json` the items were classified against. Subsection ids are positional, so a renamed or
reordered outline changes their meaning; the collector warns and `pipeline/tests/test_collector.py`
fails when the fingerprint differs from the current `taxonomy.json`, and items must then be re-filed
by `pipeline/remap_stream.py`. The script rebuilds every item's `subsections`, `failure_sources` and
`operations` from the 17 October seed classification in `pipeline/seed/items/*.json` (matched by id
or URL) through one direct map onto the current subsection list, and refuses to run when
`taxonomy.json` carries a fingerprint other than the one its map targets, so a changed outline needs
a new map before the stream can be published again.

Every item must have a working `url`, a `date`, and `verified: true` with a note; unverifiable items
are not published. `id` = `YYYY-MM-DD-` + slug of the title (ASCII, hyphens, max 80 chars), as produced
by `make_id()` in `pipeline/collect_news.py`. `date` is the event date when the source states it,
otherwise the publication date. Items are sorted by `date` descending, then `id` descending, in the
file, in `stream_recent.json` and on the stream page.
